Parallel-plate capacitor

2D electrostatics: capacitance from the field energy, fringing fields included.

Try it

open full screen ↗

Physics

Electrostatics: the potential $V$ satisfies

\[ -\nabla\cdot(\varepsilon\,\nabla V) = 0 \]

between the plates, with $V$ fixed on each. A dielectric block fills part of the gap. The stored energy is

\[ W = \tfrac12 \int \varepsilon\,|\nabla V|^2 \, dA, \]

and the capacitance follows as $C = 2W/\Delta V^2$. Because the whole box of air is solved, fringing fields are included, which is what separates the result from the textbook $\varepsilon A/d$.

Lengths are in mm and permittivities relative to $\varepsilon_0$, so $C$ comes out in units of $\varepsilon_0$ per unit depth.

Implementation in femto

The plates are a material with an enormous permittivity, so each one floats to a single voltage; one node per plate is pinned. The system is one integrate:

BasisFunction phi(V);
sparse_matrix K = integrate(dot(grad(phi), eps_q, grad(phi)), domain);

where eps_q is the per-quadrature-point permittivity. The energy is then $\tfrac12 V^T K V$.
