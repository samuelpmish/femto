TO-220 regulator on a heatsink

3D heat conduction from a silicon die through the package and heatsink to the air.

Try it

open full screen ↗

Physics

Steady heat conduction with a volumetric source in the silicon die:

\[ -\nabla\cdot(k\,\nabla T) = q, \]

and every exposed surface loses heat to the surrounding air by Newton’s law of cooling,

\[ -k\,\partial_n T = h\,(T - T_{\text{air}}). \]

The package is modeled as it is built: a copper tab with its mounting hole, the epoxy body molded around it, the silicon die soldered to the tab, and three copper leads. Three meshes compare the bare package against two heatsinks commonly clipped or bolted to its tab. Lengths are in mm, power in W, temperature in °C.

Implementation in femto

The convective term is a Robin condition: a mass-like matrix integrated over the boundary facets, added to the conduction stiffness.

sparse_matrix K  = integrate(dot(grad(phi), k_q, grad(phi)), domain);
sparse_matrix Kb = integrate(dot(phi, h_q, phi), bdr);     // h (T - T_air), the T part
Residual<Family::H1> f  = integrate(dot(q_q, phi), domain);
Residual<Family::H1> fb = integrate(dot(hT_q, phi), bdr);  // ... and the T_air part

The tetrahedral meshes come from tools/generate_example_meshes.py, and elements are sorted into copper, epoxy, silicon and aluminum by the same box tests the generator used.
