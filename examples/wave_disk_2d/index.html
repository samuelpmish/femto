Pressure pulse in a disk

2D acoustics against the analytic solution: hard wall, absorbing condition, or a PML ring.

Physics

A pressure pulse set off inside the unit disk should spread out and leave, as it does in the analytic solution of the wave equation

\[ \partial_t^2 u = c^2\,\Delta u \]

on the whole plane. A finite mesh has to stop somewhere, and what happens at its rim is the point of the example. Three treatments are compared:

  • hard wall, the natural boundary condition, which echoes the pulse straight back;
  • the first-order absorbing boundary condition of Bayliss and Turkel, which lets the parts arriving head-on leave and reflects the rest;
  • a ring of perfectly matched layer elements, which absorbs nearly everything.

The reference is the Hankel transform of the Gaussian pulse, a sum of Bessel modes with $\cos(ckt)$ time dependence.

Implementation in femto

P1 triangles or Q1 quadrilaterals from Mesh::disk, with the PML ring extruded from the rim. The mass matrix is lumped and time stepping is SSP-RK3, so each stage is just a residual evaluation:

r = integrate(dot(flux_q, grad(phi)), domain);

The PML is ScalarPML<2> from materials/pml.hpp, using the Grote–Sim auxiliary system with one auxiliary vector per quadrature point.
